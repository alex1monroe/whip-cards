Whip illustration. UPDATE, Iowa detonation: Army detonates Iowa lead azide. The nation's entire stockpile, about 5 tons, was blown up Wednesday morning at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, AP reports. Timeline: Nov. 23, the 8th Circuit denies environmental groups' emergency stay and lifts its pause on the EPA permit. Nov. 24, Gov. Reynolds declares a disaster emergency in three counties and a FEMA alert tells people 5 to 20 miles away to stay indoors. Nov. 25, the Army detonates the degraded lead azide; a plume is observed. Next, the EPA keeps testing air, soil and water, and evacuated residents get a call when it's all clear. Officials said the material risked exploding on its own as temperatures dropped. The EPA collected more than 1,200 samples and detected no lead in the air before the blast. Environmentalists raised health and environmental concerns. Sources: AP; EPA; U.S. Army; 8th Circuit; Iowa governor's office; WQAD.
