Whip illustration. Headline: "NYC rent freeze stands." A Manhattan judge upheld the Rent Guidelines Board's two-year rent freeze and dismissed a landlord lawsuit, Gothamist reports. Big numbers: about 1 million rent-stabilized apartments covered; 0% rent increase for two years. Timeline: June 2026, the board approves the freeze, fulfilling one of Mayor Zohran Mamdani's campaign pledges. Landlords sue, calling the process a "sham" that ignored owners' rising expenses; the mayor and city lawyers deny interfering. Nov. 25, Judge Brendan Lantry dismisses the suit, saying the court lacked authority to overturn it. Lantry wrote the freeze "may have detrimental impacts" on the real estate industry. Legal Aid's Ellen Davidson: courts can't override the board. What this means: the freeze stays; Lantry called guaranteeing it without authority "irresponsible"; landlords haven't said if they'll appeal. Sources: Gothamist, The New York Times, New York Post.
