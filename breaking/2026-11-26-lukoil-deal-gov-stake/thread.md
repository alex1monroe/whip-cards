# Hook + thread outline (for approval only; not for scheduling)

Hook: The U.S. government is in the group buying a Russian oil giant's overseas assets. It's also the one that has to approve the sale.

1. Hook (above) + card. A consortium led by Todd Boehly's Eldridge, with the U.S. DFC, agreed Nov. 25 to buy Lukoil International: oil fields, refineries and gas stations in 11 countries. (AP)
2. How it's paid: no cash to Russia. The group swaps about $10B in frozen Lukoil shares, which go to an escrow account in the UAE. (Reuters, CSIS)
3. The government's piece: DFC gets an ownership stake and a share of future profits, plus a board role; Reuters says stake size is undisclosed, CSIS puts it at about 15% with no money up front. (Reuters, CSIS)
4. Who approves: Treasury's OFAC must sign off; the group has 60 days of exclusive talks; Boehly says closing could take up to a year. (Reuters, CSIS)
5. The administration's side: a DFC official says moving strategic energy assets into U.S. or allied hands "is a key policy priority of the administration," and private partners wanted the "security blanket" of a U.S. government partner. (Reuters)
6. On the Hill: Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-N.Y.) says Congress banned DFC from investing in Russia less than a year ago and calls for a halt; Sens. Shaheen (D-N.H.) and Wicker (R-Miss.) raised sanctions concerns in October. (AP, Meeks release, Senate Foreign Relations)
7. Close: Two earlier deals (Gunvor, Carlyle) died at the Treasury review stage. What should Treasury weigh this time? (Reuters, CSIS)
