# Thread draft: Who pays for AI's power? (Nov. 28, 2026)

Draft only. Not posted, scheduled or published. Needs Approver clearance, then Alex's "post". Card (portrait 2440x3092 + 16:9 3840x2160) goes on post 1.

## 1/5 (177 chars)

Electric bills & data centers is tied for No. 1 of 61 issues on Whip's voter-priority ranking, level with health care costs (polls as of Nov. 23).

So who pays for AI's power? 🧵

## 2/5 (279 chars)

Big data centers can cut millions from power bills by sharply cutting grid use in a few peak hours. Legal, but Reuters says it's "drawing scrutiny as household power bills rise."

NRG: a large Texas data center can avoid $33.4M in transmission costs by staying off-grid one hour.

## 3/5 (236 chars)

Bills are already moving. EIA: September residential prices were up 8.9% in Ohio and 8.7% in Pennsylvania from a year earlier. The U.S. average rose 0.7%.

Both states are in PJM, the largest U.S. grid. How much of that is data centers?

## 4/5 (268 chars)

The other side: a Harvard-Penn study, reported by The Washington Post, finds data centers explain about 4% of the 2020-25 rise in residential prices at the utilities studied.

Harvard Business School says inflation, fuel costs and grid upgrades drove almost all of it.

## 5/5 (266 chars)

Both can be true: that study averages utilities, while PJM's independent monitor says data centers have added $29.4 billion in power-system costs on one grid.

Who pays is still being decided: Reuters says regulators are weighing changes to how grid costs are split.
